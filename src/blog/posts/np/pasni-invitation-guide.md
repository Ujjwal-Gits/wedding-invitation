A good **pasni invitation card** tells guests four things clearly: whose pasni it is, the sait date (in both BS and AD), the time of the puja and the bhoj, and where to come. Add the parents' or grandparents' names as hosts, a contact number and a simple way to reply, and you have everything a guest needs.

This guide covers what pasni means, how the date is chosen, a checklist of what to include, and copy-ready pasni invitation wording in Nepali and English. You will also find a WhatsApp message, a reminder and practical tips for planning a celebration around a baby's naps.

## What Is Pasni (Annaprashan)?

Pasni is the rice feeding ceremony in Nepal, the day a baby eats solid food for the first time. In Sanskrit it is called annaprashan, and Newar families know it as macha janku. [Wikipedia's article on Annaprashana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaprashana) notes that this rite is still widely kept in Nepal, even as some other old rites have faded.

The day usually starts with a puja at home or at a temple, followed by the first taste of food and a family meal. In cities, it can be a small home gathering or a full party with a hall and a bhoj.

### Usual age for boys and girls

The custom most families follow is simple. [Tearfund's notes on weaning practices in Nepal](https://learn.tearfund.org/en/resources/footsteps/footsteps-51-60/footsteps-52/weaning-practices-in-nepal) describe the ceremony at five months for a girl and six months for a boy. A [Nepali family blog from Australia](https://nepaliaustralian.com/2012/05/22/nwaran-and-pasni/) gives the same pattern, adding that some families hold a girl's pasni in the seventh month.

| | Baby girl | Baby boy |
|---|---|---|
| Usual month | 5th (some families choose the 7th) | 6th |
| Who fixes the exact day | Family priest or astrologer | Family priest or astrologer |
| First food | Kheer (rice pudding) or rice | Kheer (rice pudding) or rice |

### The first taste and the choosing game

The first food is usually a little kheer, given from a silver bowl or spoon. Who feeds it first differs from family to family. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaprashana) describes the mother feeding first in Nepal, while in other homes an elder starts and then everyone takes a turn. In one [family's account of their daughter's pasni](https://nepaliaustralian.com/2015/09/04/pasni-puja-for-chhori/), the grandmother cooked the kheer and each relative fed a small spoonful.

Many families also play a choosing game. A tray is set in front of the baby with things like a book, a pen, jewellery, clay or soil, and food. Whatever the baby touches first is said, in fun, to hint at the child's future, and the [same blog](https://nepaliaustralian.com/2012/05/22/nwaran-and-pasni/) describes the whole room cheering at the choice.

> [!NOTE]
> Customs vary by community and family. Ask the elders on both sides how your family does it, then plan the invitation and timing around that.

## How the Pasni Date Is Chosen

Most families do not pick the date themselves. A family priest or astrologer reads the baby's birth chart and gives a sait, an auspicious day and time. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaprashana) notes that in Nepal the date and time are chosen by an astrologer.

Ask for the sait early, ideally soon after the baby turns four months. That gives you time to book a venue, tell relatives outside the valley and send invitations. If the priest offers two or three good days, choose the one that suits the baby's routine and the most important guests.

Once you have the sait, write down two times: the puja time and the bhoj time. Guests often only need the bhoj time, but close family will want to be there for the puja and the first taste.

## What to Include on a Pasni Invitation Card

A pasni card is shorter than a wedding card, but guests still need every practical detail. Use this checklist before you print or share anything.

| Item | What to write | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Child's name | The name the family uses | Add "our son" or "our daughter" so guests know |
| Hosts | Parents' names, or grandparents if they host | Name both parents together |
| Grandparents | Optional line, "with blessings from..." | Common when the elders are hosting |
| Date | BS and AD, plus the day of the week | Example: Shukrabar, [gate] [month] 2083 BS ([date] AD) |
| Time | Puja time and bhoj time separately | Say "onwards" for the bhoj |
| Venue | Full name, area, city | Add a map link on digital invitations |
| Bhoj | Lunch or dinner, and from what time | Helps guests plan their day |
| RSVP | Name and phone number, and a reply date | Ask how many people are coming |
| Small notes | Parking, dress, quiet timing for the baby | Keep it to one line |

Two small notes help a lot at a baby's event. First, ask guests to arrive on time for the puja, because a baby's good mood does not last forever. Second, if you would prefer no flash photography or strong perfume near the baby, say so kindly in one short line.

> [!TIP]
> Write the date both ways. Elders read the Bikram Sambat date, while younger guests and relatives abroad often check the AD date in their phone calendar.

## Pasni Invitation Wording in English

English wording works well for friends, colleagues and relatives who read English more easily. Keep it short and warm. A baby's pasni is a family celebration, so the tone can be softer than a wedding card.

```copy Formal English invitation
With joy and blessings
[Father's name] and [Mother's name]
invite you and your family to the
Pasni (Annaprashan) of our son/daughter
[Child's name]

[Day], [gate] [month] 2083 BS ([date] AD)
Puja at [time], lunch from [time] onwards
[Venue name], [Area], [City]

Kindly reply to [Name] on [phone number]
```

```copy Warm and simple English invitation
[Child's name] is ready for a first taste of rice!
Please join us for a small pasni and lunch.

[Day], [date] AD ([gate] [month] 2083 BS)
From [time]
[Venue or "at our home"], [Area]

Your blessings mean the world to us.
[Parents' names]
```

## WhatsApp Message and Reminder for a Pasni

Most guests will see your invitation on their phone first. A short personal message with the link or card picture feels warmer than a forwarded image with no words. Call the most important elders before you send anything.

```copy WhatsApp invitation message
Namaste [Name] ji,
Our [son/daughter] [Child's name] has the pasni on
[Day], [gate] [month] 2083 BS ([date] AD).
Puja at [time], lunch from [time], at [venue].
We would love you and your family to come and bless [him/her].
Here is the invitation: [link]
Please let us know how many will come.
```


## Planning Around the Baby: Guests, Timing and Size

A pasni is the baby's day, but the baby has no idea about the schedule. Planning around the child's needs keeps the day happy for everyone.

### Keep the guest list small enough

A crowded room is loud and warm, and babies tire quickly. Many families invite close relatives for the puja and a wider circle for the bhoj. If space is tight, it is fine to keep the pasni small and celebrate the first birthday with more people later.

### Fit the times around naps and feeds

Ask the parents when the baby usually sleeps and feeds, and set the puja for a time the baby is normally awake and calm. Morning often works well. Leave a short gap between the puja and the bhoj so the mother can feed the baby and rest.

### Make space for the mother and baby

Set aside a quiet room near the venue where the mother can feed, change or settle the baby. Tell close family where it is. Ask guests kindly to wash their hands before holding the baby, and keep the number of people passing the baby around small.

### Give elders and far-away relatives notice

Grandparents and the mama's side usually expect a personal call, not only a message. Relatives in other cities need enough time to travel. For family abroad, a video call during the puja lets them watch the first taste live, even from the other side of the world.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Write the RSVP date on the invitation. Even a small bhoj needs a head count for food, and replies are much easier to collect when guests know the deadline.

## Pasni, Nwaran and Other Family Ceremonies

Pasni is one of several family ceremonies in a child's early life. The first is usually nwaran, the naming ceremony, which [Wikipedia describes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nwaran) as commonly held on the eleventh day after birth. If you are planning both, you can keep the same design style for a matching set, and the [nwaran invitation page](/occasions/nwaran/) shows how that looks.

Later milestones follow the same pattern: a clear date, a sait, a family meal and a guest list. ## Final Thoughts

A pasni invitation does not need to be long. It needs the child's name, the sait in BS and AD, clear puja and bhoj times, the venue and an easy way to reply. Write it with warmth, call the elders first, and plan the day around the baby rather than the clock.
