A bratabandha invitation card should name the boy as your son, give the ceremony date in Bikram Sambat with the AD date beside it, list the times for the rites and the bhoj, and give the venue with a contact person. The date comes from your priest's sait, so fix that first, book the venue, and send invitations about three to four weeks before the day.

This guide explains the bratabandha ceremony in plain words, shows how the sait shapes your timeline, and gives copy-ready Nepali and English wording. If you want to see an online version first, open our [bratabandha invitation page](/occasions/bratabandha/).

## What the bratabandha ceremony is

Bratabandha is the Nepali form of upanayana, the sacred thread rite. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/upanayana) describes upanayana as an initiation that marks a boy's entry into student life, with a sacred thread and the Gayatri mantra at its centre. It is often called a second birth.

In Nepal, the rite brings two steps together. The [Wikipedia entry on upanayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanayana) notes that bratabandha combines chudakarma (head shaving) with the thread ceremony, followed by a first alms round to relatives. For the family, it is one of the biggest days in a son's childhood.

Ages vary by text and by family. Britannica gives a classical range of roughly 5 to 24, while a scholar interviewed by [PBS Religion and Ethics](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/religionandethics/?p=20881) says the rite usually happens between 8 and 16 and ideally takes two days. Your priest and elders will guide you on what your family follows.

> [!NOTE]
> Customs differ between communities and families. Newar Buddhist families of the Shakya and Bajracharya communities hold their own bratabandha, in which boys shave their heads and dress as monks, as [Gulf Times reported](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/383883/coming-of-age-ceremony/amp). Use this guide as a starting point and follow your own family's practice.

## The main rites of the day

Knowing the order of the rites helps you write the programme on the card. Guests want to know when to arrive for the parts they care about, usually the bhiksha and the blessings.

### Chudakarma: the first head shave

The day usually starts with chudakarma. The boy's head is shaved, and a small tuft (tuppi) is left at the crown. A [Tips Nepal guide to bratabandha](https://www.tipsnepal.com/bratabandha-why-and-how/) describes the boy's sisters receiving the hair on a plate before it is wrapped and kept aside.

### Wearing the janai

Next the boy receives the janai, the sacred thread. Britannica describes it as a loop of three twisted cotton strands, worn over the left shoulder and across the chest.
### Receiving the Gayatri mantra

The guru, usually the family priest, then gives the boy the Gayatri mantra. The Tips Nepal guide describes the guru whispering it under a cloth so that no one else hears, and the boy repeating it. In the PBS interview, the boy is then asked to chant the mantra at least three times a day.

### Bhiksha from the mother and relatives

The bhiksha is often the most emotional moment. The boy, now a student, asks for alms. Tips Nepal lists the mother first, then grandmothers, sisters, aunts and other relatives, with the maternal uncle walking beside him. The alms are later offered to the guru.

### The mama's role and the symbolic journey

The maternal uncle (mama) has a special place. In the step Tips Nepal calls the journey (deshantar), the boy sets out as if to leave home and study far away, often just a short walk to a nearby temple. His mama guides him and persuades him to come back and live with his family.

## How the sait shapes your invitation timeline

Everything on the card depends on the sait (also written sahit), the auspicious date and time. Your priest or family astrologer works it out from the boy's birth chart and the panchanga. The panchanga is the same almanac used for wedding dates, and [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/another-wedding-season-in-nepal-why-too-few-dates-for-too-many-couples-every-year.html) explains that the Nepal Panchanga Nirnayak Samiti decides festival dates and auspicious times nationally.

Some families pick Shree Panchami instead. [The Rising Nepal](https://risingnepaldaily.com/news/38921) notes that the day is regarded as auspicious for functions like marriage and bratabandha. Popular days fill venues and priests quickly, so book early.

Bratabandha celebrations have also grown. [Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/bratabandha-new-culture-youths) reported that many families now host bratabandha parties on the scale of a wedding. A bigger guest list means you need more lead time and a clear way to count who is coming.

## A bratabandha invitation timeline

This is a practical plan, counted back from the sait. Adjust it if your priest gives you a date at short notice.

| When | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| As early as possible | Ask the priest for the sait and confirm the time of the rites | Every other date depends on it |
| Within a week of the sait | Book the venue, cook or caterer, and the barber if needed | Popular days book out |
| Same week | Call the mama's family, grandparents and relatives abroad | They have roles, or need time to travel |
| 3 to 4 weeks before | Send the bratabandha invitation card to everyone | Gives guests time to plan |
| 1 to 2 weeks before | Count RSVPs and give the caterer a number | Avoids too much or too little food |
| 2 to 3 days before | Send a short reminder with the map link | People forget times and turns |
| On the morning | Share the venue location with anyone running late | Saves phone calls during the rites |

If relatives live abroad, give them the date the moment the sait is fixed. Our [guide for families abroad](/nepali-abroad/) explains how they can follow along and reply from wherever they are.

> [!TIP]
> Invite the mama and maiju in person or by phone before you send the card. He has a part in the bhiksha and the journey, so he should hear the date from you first, not from a group message.

## What to include on a bratabandha invitation card

Keep the card simple. These are the details guests look for:

1. An invocation such as ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥, if your family uses one.
2. The heading: शुभ व्रतबन्ध (many cards also write ब्रतबन्ध).
3. The parents' names and the boy's name, with चि. before it.
4. The date in BS first, with the AD date beside it.
5. A short programme: rites, bhiksha and blessings, bhoj.
6. The venue with a map link, or the home address.
7. The family names at the bottom, a contact person and an RSVP date.

The structure follows a Nepali wedding card closely. Our [wedding invitation wording guide](/blog/wedding-invitation-wording-nepali-english/) explains each part in more detail, including honorifics and BS dates.

## Bratabandha card matter in Nepali

These blocks use हजुर and honorific verbs throughout. The sample date, वि.सं. २०८३ माघ ८ गते शुक्रबार, matches Friday 22 January 2027. Replace it with your own sait and check the conversion against your patro.

```copy Formal Nepali bratabandha invitation from the parents
॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥

शुभ व्रतबन्ध

हाम्रा सुपुत्र चि. [छोराको नाम]को व्रतबन्ध (चूडाकर्म तथा उपनयन) संस्कार मिति २०८३ साल माघ ८ गते शुक्रबार शुभ साइतमा सम्पन्न हुने भएकाले उक्त शुभ अवसरमा हजुर सपरिवार उपस्थित भई चिरञ्जीवीलाई शुभ आशीर्वाद दिनुहुन हार्दिक अनुरोध गर्दछौं।

कार्यक्रम
चूडाकर्म तथा व्रतबन्ध संस्कार: बिहान ७:०० बजेदेखि
भिक्षा तथा आशीर्वाद: दिउँसो १२:०० बजे
प्रीतिभोज: बेलुका ५:०० बजेदेखि

स्थान: [घर वा भेन्यूको नाम], [ठेगाना]

दर्शनाभिलाषी
[हजुरबुबा तथा हजुरआमाको नाम]
[बुबा तथा आमाको नाम] सपरिवार
सम्पर्क: [फोन नम्बर]
```

```copy Short Nepali bratabandha invitation

हाम्रा छोरा चि. [छोराको नाम]को व्रतबन्ध २०८३ माघ ८ गते शुक्रबार हुँदै छ।

यस शुभ दिनमा हजुरको उपस्थिति र आशीर्वादको अपेक्षा गर्दछौं।

स्थान: [भेन्यूको नाम], [ठेगाना]

[बुबा तथा आमाको नाम] परिवार
```

```copy A personal note for the mama and maiju
आदरणीय मामा तथा माइजू,
[छोराको नाम]को व्रतबन्ध २०८३ माघ ८ गते शुक्रबार हुने भएको छ।
भिक्षा र देशान्तरमा हजुरहरूको साथ चाहिन्छ, त्यसैले बिहान ७:०० बजेअघि नै पाल्नुहोला।

```

## Bratabandha invitation wording in English

English versions help younger guests, colleagues and relatives abroad. Put the BS date in brackets after the AD date.

```copy Formal English bratabandha invitation
Shubha Bratabandha

Mr [Father's name] and Mrs [Mother's name] [Surname]
request the pleasure of your company with family
at the Bratabandha ceremony of their son
[Boy's name]

Friday, 22 January 2027
(Magh 8, 2083 B.S.)

Chudakarma and Bratabandha rites: from 7:00 am
Bhiksha and blessings: 12:00 noon
Bhoj: 5:00 pm onwards

Venue: [Venue name], [Address]

With best compliments from
[Grandparents' names] and family
Contact: [Phone number]
```

```copy Bilingual bratabandha invitation
शुभ व्रतबन्ध | Shubha Bratabandha

हाम्रा सुपुत्र चि. [छोराको नाम]
Our son [Boy's name]

वि.सं. २०८३ माघ ८ गते शुक्रबार
Friday, 22 January 2027

हजुर सपरिवार उपस्थित भई आशीर्वाद दिनुहुन हार्दिक अनुरोध गर्दछौं।
We warmly invite you and your family to bless him.

प्रीतिभोज | Bhoj: बेलुका ५:०० बजेदेखि | 5:00 pm onwards
स्थान | Venue: [Venue name], [Address]
```

```copy Bhoj-only invitation in English
Mr and Mrs [Parents' names]
invite you and your family to a bhoj
to celebrate the Bratabandha of their son [Boy's name]

Friday, 22 January 2027 (Magh 8, 2083 B.S.)
5:00 pm onwards
[Venue name], [Address]
Kindly reply by [date].
```

Send the link with a short personal message. Name the guest, name the boy, and say where to reply.

## Before you send

Read the card once more for the details families most often get wrong: the boy's name spelling, the BS and AD dates, the bhoj time and the venue. Ask an elder to check the Nepali, and make sure the mama's family heard the news from you first.
