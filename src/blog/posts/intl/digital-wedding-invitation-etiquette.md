**Is it OK to send wedding invitations online?** Yes, for most weddings it is. Etiquette experts now treat digital invitations as acceptable, as long as they feel personal and match the formality of your day.

The best guide is a simple test from the [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-email-dos-and-donts): if you would be comfortable extending the invitation over the phone, email is acceptable. The more formal the event, the more paper still makes sense. Most couples land somewhere in between, and that is where this guide helps.

## Is It OK to Send Wedding Invitations Online? What Experts Say

Etiquette has moved with the times, but it has not dropped its standards. The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-email-dos-and-donts) notes that just because you *can* send an email does not mean you *should*, and that formality is the deciding factor.

[The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/digital-invitation-options) presents digital invitations as a good fit for many couples. It also gives a fair warning: some guests may not think a digital invitation is proper, and some simply love a paper card they can keep.

Even digital invitation companies agree that how you send matters most. [Greenvelope](https://card.greenvelope.com/resources/are-digital-wedding-invitations-tacky), which sells online invitations, points to mass group emails as what makes an invitation feel tacky, and recommends sending each one to a named guest or household.

So the real question is not "digital or paper?" It is "how do I make a digital invitation feel like it was made for this guest?"

## When Digital Works and When to Mix in Paper

### When online wedding invitations are a great fit

**Online wedding invitations** work especially well in these cases:

- A relaxed or semi-formal wedding, such as a garden party or a small dinner
- Guests spread across several cities or countries
- A multi-day celebration with several events, like a mehndi, sangeet and reception
- A short timeline, where mail would eat up weeks
- A couple who wants RSVPs, maps and updates in one place

### When to keep paper, or mix both

Paper still earns its place for a very formal or black-tie wedding. It also matters for guests who do not use email or smartphones much, and for anyone who will treasure a card in a frame or album.

Many couples do both. They mail a small number of printed invitations to grandparents and older relatives, and send the digital version to everyone else. This keeps the keepsake for the people who value it most, without printing and posting 150 cards.

> [!TIP]
> Ask your parents or the oldest people on your list how they would like to be invited. One five-minute call can save hurt feelings later.

## Digital Wedding Invitation Etiquette for Addressing Guests

Addressing is where **digital wedding invitation etiquette** matters most. On paper, the envelope tells guests exactly who is invited. Online, your message and the invitation itself have to do that job.

### Names and households

Send each invitation to one person or one household. Use the names you would write on an envelope: "Dear Maria and James," not "Hi all." Never put your whole guest list in one email's To line, and never add guests to a group chat to invite them.

If your invitation tool supports personal links, use them. With Nimtto, personal guest links (each guest's own name on their invitation) are part of the [Pro Max plan](/international/pricing/).

### Plus-ones

Be specific. If a guest may bring someone, say so plainly in your message, and name the partner if you know them. If a guest is invited alone, address the message to them only, and do not mention a plus-one at all.

### Children and adults-only weddings

The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/how-to-signal-no-children-at-your-wedding) explains that the way an invitation is addressed shows exactly who is invited and, by leaving names out, who is not. It advises against writing "no children" on the invitation itself.

When children are invited, the [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/addressing-sending-wedding-invitations/) suggests writing their names on a line below their parents' names. Online, that simply means naming each child in your message or on the invitation. For an adults-only wedding, address only the adults, and put a gentle note on your wedding website or in a personal message to parents.

```copy Plus-one wording
Dear [Name],
We would love for you and a guest to celebrate with us on [date].
When you RSVP, please add your guest's full name so we can include them on our seating plan.
With love,
[Your names]
```

```copy Adults-only wording (personal message to parents)
Hi [Name],
We are so happy you and [Partner's name] can join us on [date].
We have planned our celebration as an adults-only evening, and we hope this gives you a relaxed night out.
Thank you for understanding. We cannot wait to see you both.
[Your names]
```

## Timing: When to Send Online Wedding Invitations

Digital invitations arrive instantly, but guests still need the same notice. [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/wedding-invitations-etiquette) suggests sending invitations six to eight weeks before the wedding. Destination weddings and multi-day events need more notice, because guests have to plan travel and time off.

A common plan looks like this:

1. **Save the date:** several months ahead, so guests can block the dates
2. **Invitation:** about six to eight weeks ahead, with the full details and RSVP
3. **RSVP deadline:** a few weeks before the wedding, and before your caterer's final count is due
4. **Reminder:** about a week before the RSVP deadline, only to guests who have not replied

## What to Include (and What to Leave on the Website)

Keep the invitation itself focused. [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/wedding-invitations-etiquette) recommends sticking to the essential details on the invitation and putting everything else on your wedding website.

A clear digital invitation includes:

- Your names, and the hosts' names if your parents are hosting
- The date and start time of each event
- The venue, with a map link
- Dress code, if you have one
- How and when to RSVP

The extras belong one tap away: hotel blocks, travel notes, parking, your story and photos. A digital invitation can hold all of this on one page. A Nimtto invitation, for example, includes every event with its time, venue and Google Maps link, plus travel notes and RSVP, as shown on the [features](/international/features/) page.

### Registry mention etiquette

This is one rule that has not changed. The [Emily Post Institute](https://emilypost.com/advice/inside-weddings-registry-rules) says a wedding invitation should never mention gifts or registry details, and that the wedding website is the right home for that information.

Online, follow the same spirit. Do not put a registry link in your invitation message. Let guests find it on your wedding website, or share it when someone asks. Nimtto does not run gift registries, so if you have one, link to it from the website section that guests explore on their own.

## The Message to Send With Your Link

The note you send with the link is your envelope. It should be warm, short and personal. Our full guide to [wedding invitation wording examples](/international/blog/wedding-invitation-wording-examples/) has invitation text for every style, but here is the message itself.

```copy Message to send with the invitation link
Dear [Name],
We are getting married! We would be so happy to have you with us on [date] in [city].
Your invitation, with all the details and the RSVP, is here:
[link]
Please reply by [RSVP date]. We hope to see you there.
With love,
[Your names]
```

```copy Note to an older relative, offering a printed copy
Dear [Aunt / Uncle / Grandma Name],
We sent our wedding invitation by email, but we would love for you to have a printed copy too.
We will mail one to you this week. If it is easier, you can reply by phone at [number].
We cannot wait to celebrate with you.
Love,
[Your names]
```

> [!NOTE]
> Send from your own email or phone number, with a clear subject line such as "Our wedding invitation: [Your names]." A message from an unknown sender is easy to ignore or lose to spam.

## RSVP Deadline Etiquette and Polite Follow-Ups

Guests should reply by your deadline. That is basic etiquette for them. Your part is to make replying easy and the date impossible to miss.

Put the RSVP date in your message and on the invitation. Make the reply button easy to find. If guests can answer for several events, let them choose each one separately, so your mehndi count and your reception count stay accurate.

When the deadline passes, follow up kindly. Assume a busy week, not rudeness. Start with a short message, and call only if you still have not heard back a few days later. Our guide to [collecting RSVPs online](/international/blog/how-to-collect-rsvps-online/) explains how to track replies and export your final list.

```copy Gentle RSVP reminder
Hi [Name]! Just a friendly note that we are finalizing numbers for [date].
If you have a moment, could you RSVP here by [new date]? [link]
No pressure at all if you cannot make it. We would just love to know.
Thank you!
[Your names]
```

## Privacy and Changes of Plans

### Keep the link off social media

An online invitation is still a private invitation. Do not post the link on Instagram, Facebook or a public story. Anyone who sees it can open it, and you may get replies from people you did not plan to host.

Share it only one-to-one or one-to-household. Ask close family not to forward it. If you want to celebrate publicly, post a photo of the design without the link.

### When plans change

This is where **paperless wedding invitations** shine. If the venue, time or date changes, you update the invitation once and the same link shows the new details. No reprinting, and no second mailing.

Still tell guests directly. People will not open the link again unless something prompts them.

```copy Change-of-plans update
Hi [Name],
A quick update for our wedding: [the reception will now start at 7:00 pm / the venue has changed to New Venue].
The invitation has been updated with the new details and map: [link]
Your RSVP still stands, so there is nothing else you need to do.
Thank you, and see you soon!
[Your names]
```

## E-Vite Wedding Etiquette: Do's and Don'ts

Here is a quick reference for **e-vite wedding etiquette**, so you can check your plan before you hit send.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Send to each guest or household by name | Send one mass email or group chat message |
| Match the design to how formal your wedding is | Use a casual template for a black-tie event |
| Say clearly who is invited, including plus-ones and children | Leave guests guessing whether they can bring someone |
| Put the RSVP date in your message and on the invitation | Hide the reply button below long text |
| Offer printed copies to older guests who prefer paper | Assume everyone checks email every day |
| Keep registry details on your website | Put a registry link in the invitation message |
| Keep the link private | Post the link on social media |
| Message guests when details change | Change the invitation quietly and hope they notice |
| Send thank-you notes after the wedding | Skip thank-yous because the invitation was digital |

## Final Thoughts

Sending your wedding invitation online is good etiquette when you treat it like paper: addressed to a person, clear about who is invited, and sent with care. Add printed copies for the few guests who will treasure them, and you have covered everyone.
