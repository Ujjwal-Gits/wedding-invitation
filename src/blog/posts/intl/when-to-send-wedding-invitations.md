The usual answer to when to send wedding invitations is six to eight weeks before a local wedding, with save the dates going out six to eight months before. Set your RSVP deadline about three to four weeks before the wedding. That is the common guidance from [Emily Post](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-planning-series) and The Knot.

For a destination wedding, a holiday weekend, or many guests flying in from abroad, add time at every step: save the dates eight to twelve months out, and invitations two to four months out. The rest of this guide explains why, and what to do if you are already behind.

## The Short Answer: When to Send Wedding Invitations

Here is the standard plan for a local wedding, with the source for each step.

- **Save the dates: 6 to 8 months before.** [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/save-the-dates-etiquette) suggests this window and says no later than six months if you can. Emily Post's planning timeline is a little looser, starting at three to six months or earlier.
- **Invitations: 6 to 8 weeks before.** Both [Emily Post](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-planning-series) and The Knot use this window.
- **RSVP deadline: 3 to 4 weeks before.** [The Knot's RSVP guide](https://www.theknot.com/content/rsvp-time-guest-follow-up) says at least four weeks out is ideal and no later than two weeks.

Why these numbers? Six to eight weeks gives guests about a month to reply. You then have a few weeks to give your caterer a final count and finish the seating plan.

## Wedding Invitation Timeline, Month by Month

Use this wedding invitation timeline as a checklist. The right-hand column shows how things shift when guests travel.

| When | Local wedding | Destination or travel-heavy wedding |
|---|---|---|
| 12 to 9 months before | Set the date, start the guest list | Send save the dates (8 to 12 months is common) |
| 8 to 6 months before | Send save the dates | Wedding website live with travel and hotel notes |
| 5 to 4 months before | Wedding website live; choose your invitation design | Earliest window for invitations if travel is hard |
| 3 months before | Finalize the guest list and addresses | Send invitations (about 10 to 12 weeks or earlier) |
| 10 to 8 weeks before | Prepare invitations; send early to far-away guests | RSVP deadline for remote or peak-season trips |
| 8 to 6 weeks before | Send invitations | First reminder to guests who have not replied |
| 5 weeks before | First RSVP reminder (one week before the deadline) | Confirm counts for hotels and transport |
| 4 to 3 weeks before | RSVP deadline | Final reminders and calls |
| 3 to 2 weeks before | Final reminder, then phone the stragglers | Give final numbers to vendors |
| 1 week before | Send an update message with last details | Send an update message with arrival details |

The destination column draws on The Knot's guidance on [destination wedding invitations](https://www.theknot.com/content/destination-wedding-invitation-etiquette): save the dates eight to twelve months out, invitations closer to ten or twelve weeks, and an RSVP deadline at least eight to ten weeks before for planners who need hotel numbers early. Some planners quoted there send invitations four to six months ahead so guests can book rooms and ask for time off.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Anyone who receives a save the date must also receive an invitation, as [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/save-the-dates-etiquette) points out. Only send save the dates once your guest list is firm.

## When to Send Save the Dates

Save the dates are optional, but they matter most when guests must travel, book leave or plan around a busy season. They only need your names, the date, the city, and a note that an invitation will follow. You do not need final venue details yet.

Do not go too early, though. The Knot advises against sending them more than a year ahead, because guests tend to forget or lose the details.

## Special Cases: Destination, Holiday and International Guests

The standard numbers assume most guests live nearby. Adjust them for these situations. The local and destination rows follow the sources in this guide; the other rows are our practical suggestions built from them.

| Situation | Save the dates | Invitations | RSVP due |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local wedding | 6 to 8 months | 6 to 8 weeks | 3 to 4 weeks before |
| Holiday weekend | Closer to 8 months | 8 weeks or a little earlier | 4 weeks before |
| Destination wedding | 8 to 12 months | 10 to 12 weeks, or up to 4 to 6 months | 8 to 10 weeks before |
| Many guests from abroad | 9 to 12 months | 3 to 4 months | 6 to 8 weeks before |
| Multi-event weekend | Same as the main wedding | One invitation with every event | Same deadline for all events |

### Holiday weekends

Flights and hotels fill up fast around long weekends. The Knot's [save the date guide](https://www.theknot.com/content/save-the-dates-etiquette) suggests leaning toward eight months for a holiday weekend or a far-flung location.

### Destination wedding invitation timeline

Guests may need to book flights, rooms and time off, and sometimes renew a passport. That is why the destination wedding invitation timeline starts so early, and why the RSVP date is earlier too. Hotel room blocks often have their own release dates, so check your contracts and set your deadline before them.

### International guests

Guests crossing borders need paperwork time. The [U.S. State Department](https://travel.state.gov/en/passports/apply/help/processing-time.html) lists routine passport processing at about four to six weeks, plus mailing time. Guests who need a visa should check the [Global Visa Wait Times](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/global-visa-wait-times.html) page for their nearest embassy, because waits vary by city and the estimate does not include processing. Send these guests their save the date as early as you can, and tell them the date personally.

### Multi-event weddings

If you are hosting a welcome dinner, the ceremony and a farewell brunch, [Zola](https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/wedding-weekend-events-what-to-include-in-your-main-invitation-vs-enclosure) recommends listing them together on an itinerary when every guest is invited to all of them. Send it all at once, with one RSVP date, and ask guests to reply for each event so you get a separate count for every meal.

## How Digital Invitations Change the Timing

An online invitation removes printing, addressing and postage time. Your send date can be the date you planned, instead of two weeks earlier to allow for the mail. Late changes are easier too, because you can update the details instead of reprinting.

What does not change is your guests' side. They still need several weeks to check calendars, arrange childcare or book travel. So keep the same six to eight week window for a local wedding, and do not send much earlier just because you can. An invitation that arrives too early is easy to forget.

## RSVP Deadline and Reminder Cadence

Work backward from your caterer's final headcount date. Set the RSVP deadline a week or two before it, so you have time to chase late replies.

A simple reminder rhythm:

1. **One week before the deadline:** a friendly first reminder to anyone who has not replied. [Greenvelope](https://www.greenvelope.com/resources/when-to-send-wedding-invitations-and-save-the-dates) suggests this timing.
2. **One or two days after the deadline:** a final reminder, also Greenvelope's suggestion.
3. **About two to three weeks before the wedding:** phone anyone still missing. [Emily Post](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-planning-series) recommends calling stragglers around this point.

Three touches is enough. More than that starts to feel like pressure.

## What to Do If You Are Running Late

Being late is common and fixable. The goal is to give guests as much notice as you can and make replying easy.

- **Tell close family and key guests first.** A personal call or message the same day you set the date is worth more than any card.
- **Skip the save the date** if the wedding is less than four months away. Send the invitation instead.
- **Shorten the reply window, not the notice.** Keep at least two to three weeks for replies if you can.
- **Choose a format that sends fast.** An online invitation goes out the day it is ready. Nimtto's design help usually takes one to three business days.

[Emily Post](https://emilypost.com/advice/wedding-planning-series) also notes that if you invite a second group of guests later, they should still get at least four weeks to reply.

### If your wedding is in X weeks

- **16 weeks or more:** you are on time. Send a save the date now if guests travel, invitations at 6 to 8 weeks.
- **12 weeks:** skip save the dates for local guests. Send invitations to far-away guests now, everyone else in a month.
- **8 weeks:** send all invitations this week, RSVP due in four weeks.
- **6 weeks:** send today, RSVP due in three weeks, and call family personally.
- **4 weeks or less:** call or message everyone, send the invitation the same day, and ask for replies within one to two weeks.

## Wording to Copy for Every Step

```copy Save the date message
Save the date!
Emma and James are getting married on Saturday, June 12, 2027, in Portland, Oregon.
Invitation and details to follow. We hope you can make it!
```

```copy Invitation message
Hi [Guest's name]!
Our wedding is on Saturday, June 12, 2027, and we'd love for you to celebrate with us.
Here's the invitation with times, venues and maps: [link]
Please reply on the invitation by May 15.
Love, Emma and James
```

```copy RSVP reminder 1
Hi [Guest's name], just a friendly reminder to reply to our wedding invitation by May 15.
It only takes a minute: [link]
We hope to see you there!
```

```copy Final RSVP reminder
Hi [Guest's name], we're giving our final numbers to the venue this week.
Could you let us know if you can make it by Friday? Here's the link again: [link]
Thank you so much!
```

```copy Update message
Hi [Guest's name], a quick update for our wedding on June 12:
the ceremony now starts at 5:00 p.m. instead of 4:30 p.m.
Everything else stays the same. Full details: [link]
```

```copy "We missed your reply" note
Hi [Guest's name], we haven't seen your reply yet and didn't want you to miss out!
Will you be able to join us on June 12? A quick yes or no is perfect.
Here's the invitation: [link]
```

## Final Thoughts

Timing is about your guests' plans, not the stationery. Give people enough notice to arrange their lives, a clear date to reply by, and a gentle nudge or two. Do that and your count will be ready when your caterer asks.
