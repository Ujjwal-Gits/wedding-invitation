# Jhyal (झ्याल) · the carved Newari window

Page: `/jhyal/` (demo) · `/jhyal/?id=<invitation>` (studio data) · dev server on 5190.

## Concept
Clean, minimal and premium: one carved sal-wood Newari window (aankhijhyal / sanjhyal style) on ivory lokta paper.
Warm wood browns (never black), sindoor red as the one accent and only in small touches (the invocation, rules,
the ampersand, the programme line, the folded lagan patra), a little antique gold on bosses and fillets.
No big red slabs, no gold foil, no Ganesh art: the window carries the tradition.

Type: **Tiro Devanagari Hindi** (शुभ विवाह, Nepali names and headings), **Noto Serif Devanagari** 300 (thin Nepali
numerals in the date and countdown), **Newsreader** (Latin names and headings, 300 for thin numerals), **Mukta** (all
reading text). base.css keeps `locl` off, so ८ reads correctly. Devanagari is never tracked or uppercased.

## Art (no AI images)
- `art.js` draws the window from one geometry (`windowGeom`): a cusped **torana** with a half lotus, vine scrolls and
  a gilt finial; an extended **lintel** and **sill** with scrolled wings and carved rosettes; a frame of moulding,
  bead-and-reel, a gilt fillet and lotus petals; hanging dentils; **lattice shutters** (`shutterSVG`, a 45° pattern
  with gilt knots, carved panels above and below, slim meeting stiles so the names behind stay legible). The same
  geometry frames the main photo (`photoWindow`, built at the photo's own shape so it is never cropped) and the
  closing window. Also the brass diyo, the carved pillar (capital, beaded shaft tile, base), the Dhaka strip, the
  lotus divider and node, and the petals.
- `public/themes/jhyal/paper.webp`: seamless lokta paper, generated by `tools/paper.py`.
- `public/themes/jhyal/decor/`: cut-outs copied from Mangal (toran, kalash), Mala (photo corner) and Phoolbari (sprig);
  sources in `flora-credits.js`. Flowers appear only four times: the toran over the window, the kalash, one photo
  corner, one section divider. Never over text.

## Signature motion: the window opens
The closed window fits one screen at every size (sized from width and height in `layoutGate`); through the lattice
the couple's names show faintly. Tap "झ्याल खोल्नुहोस् / Open the window" (or "Open without music"): the latch gives,
the two shutters swing outward in 3D (CSS perspective, transform/opacity only, about 1.2 s on a custom ease), soft
morning light fills the opening and a few marigold and rose petals drift out toward the guest, then the view moves
through the window (`throughScale()` finds the scale at which the opening covers the screen) and the page settles in.
Median frame 16.7 ms on the GPU. Reduced motion: a crossfade; the lagan patra is shown open.

## Sections
1. **Hero:** ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥ (small, sindoor; optional), शुभ विवाह, the names, one warm line, and the main photo
   inside a smaller carved window (side by side on desktop, stacked on phones).
2. **The date:** a folded red **lagan patra** tied with a gold thread; it unfolds (tap, button, or by itself once in
   view) to the weekday, the date (AD in English, B.S. in Nepali, the other below), `timeLabel` and the venue.
   Then **शुभ साइत आउन बाँकी** with thin numerals (Nepali digits in NE).
3. **Families:** two columns either side of a carved wooden pillar with a kalash on top (a carved beam on phones).
4. **The two of us:** a calm masonry grid (3 columns desktop, 2 on phones, 1 below 380 px) of uncropped photos in
   thin wooden-lined mounts; tap for the lightbox.
5. **Card wording** on handmade paper between two thin Dhaka strips, with the full-invitation modal.
6. **Programme:** a vertical timeline on a thin sindoor line: name, AD + B.S. date, time, venue + map, dress if given.
7. **RSVP** (`core/rsvp.js`), then the **closing window**, open, with dawn light and two diyos, the blessing, the
   names, the hashtag, Made with Nimtto and a Dhaka strip.
